# 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the required sequence of clinical and administrative approvals for every patient referral handled by the Home Health division, and the process for escalating non-responding wounds to the Stem Cell Wound Care division. Its goals are to:

- Ensure every episode of care is authorized by the correct people, in the correct order, before care begins or changes.

- Keep patient information consistent and identical across every approver — one record, one source of truth.

- Create a complete, timestamped audit trail of who approved what, and when.

- Standardize the process so it can be executed the same way at any office and, eventually, licensed to other agencies.

# 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all referrals for skilled home health services (nursing visits, rehabilitation, medication management, standard wound care) and all escalations to advanced stem cell wound care. It covers the approval workflow only; clinical treatment protocols are governed by separate clinical policies.

# 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |

| Case | A single patient referral and everything attached to it: diagnosis, orders, notes, approvals, and escalations. Each case has a unique Case ID. |

# 5. Procedure — Standard Home Health Referral

## 5.1 Case creation

- The Doctor opens a new case containing, at minimum: patient name, diagnosis / reason for referral, and an order note with care instructions and visit frequency.

- Supporting documentation is attached at creation where applicable (e.g., wound photographs for any wound-related referral, discharge summary for post-surgical cases). A wound-related referral without photos must be sent back by the Head Nurse.

- Submission by the Doctor counts as the first sign-off in the chain.

## 5.2 Sequential approval

Approvals must occur in order. A stage cannot be acted on until every stage before it is approved. The order is:

**Doctor → Head Nurse → Head of Staff → Assigned Nurse**

- The system notifies the next approver the moment the preceding stage is signed off.

- Each approver reviews the same, single case record. Information is never re-entered or transcribed between stages.

- Each approver either approves (note optional) or sends the case back (written reason required).

- Upon the Assigned Nurse's sign-off, the case status becomes Active and scheduled care begins.

## 5.3 Send-backs and resubmission

- A send-back stops the chain immediately. The case status becomes Needs Revision.

- The send-back reason must state specifically what is missing or incorrect.

- The Doctor revises the case and resubmits. Resubmission restarts the chain at the stage that issued the send-back; earlier approvals remain valid unless the revision changed clinical content, in which case the full chain restarts. [Confirm this rule with leadership.]

- All send-backs and resubmissions remain permanently visible in the case history.

# 6. Procedure — Escalation to Stem Cell Wound Care

## 6.1 When to escalate

A case is eligible for escalation when standard home health wound care is not achieving expected healing progress. Placeholder clinical criteria (to be defined by the Medical Director):

- [No measurable improvement after X weeks of standard wound care]

- [Wound classified at or above stage X]

- [Signs of infection or deterioration documented on X consecutive visits]

## 6.2 Who may escalate

Only the Doctor or the Head Nurse may initiate an escalation, and only after the home health approval chain is fully approved.

## 6.3 Escalation process

- The initiator triggers escalation on the existing case. The case is not duplicated — the full home health history travels with it.

- Escalation opens a new approval chain for the Wound Care business line, in the same order: Doctor → Head Nurse → Head of Staff → Assigned Nurse.

- The wound care chain requires its own fresh sign-offs at every stage. No approval carries over from the home health chain. [Confirm with leadership.]

- Home health services continue or pause during wound care review per the Doctor's order on the escalation note. [Define default.]

## 7.2 Time and notes are one submission

Every visit is recorded in the case record with a clock-in time, clock-out time, and visit notes.
A time entry cannot be submitted without visit notes. Time and notes are submitted together as a single entry; the system will not accept one without the other.
A nurse with an open (unsubmitted) visit may not start another visit on any case until the open visit's time and notes are submitted.

# 8. Case Record and Data Integrity

- One record per case. All roles view and act on the same record; no side copies, printouts used as working documents, or re-keyed data.

- Append-only history. Sign-offs, send-backs, and notes are never edited or deleted. Corrections are made by adding a new entry.

- Every entry is attributed (role + individual) and timestamped automatically.

- Required fields per case: Case ID, patient name, diagnosis/reason, business line(s), current stage, full approval history. [Add insurance/authorization fields as required.]

- Attachments (photos, discharge summaries, lab results) are uploaded to the case record, not sent by email or text.
